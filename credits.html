Credits and sources

PQC Wonderland is a post-quantum cryptography tutorial and classroom by Ridley DiSiena. It teaches published standards and published research; this page lists that work, the software and fonts the page ships with, and their licences.

◂ back to PQC Wonderland

Standards the scenes implement or follow

  • FIPS 203, Module-Lattice-Based Key-Encapsulation Mechanism Standard (ML-KEM), NIST, 2024. csrc.nist.gov
  • FIPS 204, Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Standard (ML-DSA), NIST, 2024. csrc.nist.gov
  • FIPS 205, Stateless Hash-Based Digital Signature Standard (SLH-DSA), NIST, 2024. csrc.nist.gov
  • FIPS 206 (draft), FN-DSA, and the Falcon specification it is based on: Fouque, Hoffstein, Kirchner, Lyubashevsky, Pornin, Prest, Ricosset, Seiler, Whyte and Zhang, Falcon: Fast-Fourier Lattice-based Compact Signatures over NTRU, specification v1.2. falcon-sign.info
  • HQC (selected by NIST in March 2025; the future FIPS 207): Aguilar Melchor, Aragon, Bettaieb, Bidoux, Blazy, Deneuville, Gaborit, Persichetti, Zémor and others, Hamming Quasi-Cyclic (HQC), round 4 specification of 22 August 2025, and the submitters' reference implementation v5.0.0, whose known-answer test values the classroom reproduces. pqc-hqc.org
  • NIST IR 8545, Status Report on the Fourth Round of the NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography Standardization Process, 2025. csrc.nist.gov
  • NIST IR 8547, Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards, October 2026. csrc.nist.gov
  • NIST PQC Call for Proposals (2016), §4.A.5, the security categories. csrc.nist.gov
  • FIPS 202 (SHA-3 and SHAKE), FIPS 180-4 (SHA-2), FIPS 186-5 (digital signatures), SP 800-208 (stateful hash-based signatures, LMS and XMSS), SP 800-57 (key management), NIST.

IETF documents

  • RFC 9180, Hybrid Public Key Encryption (the DHKEM used for X25519 and the NIST curves). rfc-editor.org
  • RFC 8017, PKCS #1 v2.2 (RSA-OAEP). rfc-editor.org
  • RFC 5280 (X.509 certificates), RFC 5480 and RFC 8410 (elliptic-curve and Edwards keys in certificates), RFC 4211 (CRMF, proof of possession by challenge), RFC 2986 (PKCS #10).
  • RFC 5869 (HKDF), RFC 2104 (HMAC), RFC 3394 (AES Key Wrap), RFC 9629 (KEM algorithms in CMS). rfc-editor.org
  • RFC 6962 and RFC 9162, Certificate Transparency (the Merkle tree hashing and proofs). rfc-editor.org
  • RFC 7748 (X25519, X448), RFC 8032 (EdDSA), RFC 6979 (deterministic ECDSA), RFC 8391 (XMSS), RFC 8446 (TLS 1.3), RFC 10024 (the hybrid X25519MLKEM768 group, as cited in the Both Pills scene).
  • X-Wing: Barbosa, Connolly, Duarte, Kaiser, Schwabe, Varner and Westerbaan, draft-connolly-cfrg-xwing-kem. datatracker.ietf.org
  • Hybrid KEMs (the QSF construction), draft-irtf-cfrg-hybrid-kems. datatracker.ietf.org
  • KEM-based authentication in IKEv2, draft-wang-ipsecme-kem-auth-ikev2 (the Tea Party's mutual mode). datatracker.ietf.org
  • LAMPS and PLANTS drafts referenced in the lessons: draft-ietf-lamps-kyber-certificates, draft-ietf-lamps-cms-kyber, draft-massimo-lamps-hqc-certificates, draft-ietf-plants-merkle-tree-certs.

Research the lessons teach

  • Regev, “On lattices, learning with errors, random linear codes, and cryptography”, STOC 2005; J. ACM 56(6), 2009. (Lessons 2–4.)
  • Fujisaki and Okamoto, “Secure integration of asymmetric and symmetric encryption schemes”, CRYPTO 1999; and Hofheinz, Hövelmanns and Kiltz, “A modular analysis of the Fujisaki–Okamoto transformation”, TCC 2017. (The FO transform, lessons 4 and 12.)
  • Lyubashevsky, “Fiat–Shamir with aborts”, ASIACRYPT 2009, and “Lattice signatures without trapdoors”, EUROCRYPT 2012. (Rejection sampling, lesson 5.)
  • Lamport, “Constructing digital signatures from a one way function”, SRI CSL-98, 1979; Merkle, “A certified digital signature”, CRYPTO 1989; Hülsing, “W-OTS+: shorter signatures for hash-based signature schemes”, AFRICACRYPT 2013. (Lessons 7–9.)
  • Bernstein, Hülsing, Kölbl, Niederhagen, Rijneveld and Schwabe, “The SPHINCS+ signature framework”, ACM CCS 2019. (SLH-DSA, lesson 9.)
  • Gentry, Peikert and Vaikuntanathan, “Trapdoors for hard lattices and new cryptographic constructions”, STOC 2008. (Falcon as trapdoor sampling, lesson 10.)
  • Reed, Muller (1954) and Reed and Solomon (1960) on the codes HQC concatenates. (Lesson 12.)
  • Shor, “Algorithms for quantum computation: discrete logarithms and factoring”, FOCS 1994; Grover, “A fast quantum mechanical algorithm for database search”, STOC 1996. (Lessons 11 and 13.)
  • Schwabe, Stebila and Wiggers, “Post-quantum TLS without handshake signatures” (KEMTLS), ACM CCS 2020. (The Tea Party's idea of authenticating with a KEM.)
  • Lagrange (1773) and Gauss (1801) for two-dimensional lattice reduction, the classroom's Expert bench 1.
